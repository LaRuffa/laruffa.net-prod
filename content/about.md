## Hi, I'm Mike

I'm an IT and data professional who loves to learn and keep growing. I started at a casino resort, resetting passwords and terminating CAT6. Within two years I was the only systems administrator at a brand-new property, standing up its whole IT environment from an empty server room.

In 2026 I moved into a **Marketing Analyst** role at Resorts World Catskills. There I use SQL, Excel and Power Query to report on promotions and check the numbers against the database. Running the systems taught me how the data is produced; analyzing it teaches me what the business needs from it.

I've earned my **AZ-900** and I'm working toward **AZ-104** (Azure Administrator). I enjoy work that uses both sides: infrastructure, identity and automation, plus the data that runs through them.

## What I bring

- **Ownership.** I've been the only person on call, so I document, automate and fix root causes instead of symptoms.
- **Calm under pressure.** During the global CrowdStrike outage, I had servers and front-line PCs back up within hours.
- **Security mindset.** I've rolled out MFA, isolated a ransomware attack, and managed vendor access through least-privilege tools.
- **Business awareness.** I sat in weekly operations meetings on budgets and expansion, and now I report on marketing performance, so I know IT exists to keep the business running.
- **Data I can trust.** I check reports against the source database and automate data-quality checks with PowerShell.

## The homelab

Most of what I learn starts at home. Everything runs behind a **pfSense** firewall (easy to use, secure, and I love it). A **WireGuard** tunnel to a RackNerd VPS publishes services without opening ports at home. Game servers run under **CubeCoders AMP** (the Application Management Panel, not Cisco's endpoint product). I bought the Ubiquiti switch before I trained on Cisco and don't regret it.

## Linux

Like most techies, I've gone down the Linux route. Windows is my daily driver, but I have intermediate experience with:

- **Ubuntu**: my favorite. Stable, and it works out of the box for what I need.
- **RHEL**: used both professionally and personally, with no major complaints.
- **Arch**: great with Hyprland, but getting it the way I want takes more time than I have.
- **Kali**: we've all had the "hacker phase." These days I'd rather install the tools I need on Ubuntu or Arch.
- **Mint**: sweet mint.
- **Pi OS**: honestly, my least favorite.
